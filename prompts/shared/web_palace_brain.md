# Shared Web Palace brain

A Web Palace is a teaching-first digital experience that helps a defined learner move from a known foundation to a demonstrable target capability. It is not a decorative landing page, a shallow course outline, or a collection of disconnected cards.

Preserve a coherent foundation-to-target journey. The requested difficulty is the ceiling, not the assumed starting point. Establish what the learner knows, can do, recognises intuitively, and misunderstands. Sequence concepts by dependency, give one recommended first route, and finish with an integrated capability that transfers to a changed situation.

For every substantial concept, preserve this teaching chain across the route:

1. recognisable situation;
2. plain-language meaning and purpose;
3. formal name;
4. parts and mechanism;
5. small guided example with progressively revealed information;
6. interpretation and limits;
7. a manageable learner prediction, calculation, classification, or decision;
8. corrective feedback for sound and mistaken reasoning;
9. a boundary case or misconception;
10. a reason to continue to the next concept.

Prefer one coherent explanation over disconnected slogans or generic cards. Introduce a technical term only after explaining its underlying idea. Each route must identify what is assumed before it and what the learner understands or can do afterward; later routes must deepen rather than repeat the introduction.

Every implementation must provide meaningful content depth, usable sequential navigation, responsive behavior, keyboard access, readable contrast, reduced-motion support, textual alternatives for complex visuals, and explanatory feedback for interactive elements. Present the full route architecture no more than once on the landing experience. Visual polish must support comprehension rather than substitute for it.

Derive visuals and interactions from the current subject. A visual must clarify hierarchy, sequence, causality, comparison, state, or practice. An interaction must answer a learner question, define its state and feedback, work with keyboard and touch, respect reduced motion, and retain a static fallback. Do not copy another Web Palace's page structure or visual identity mechanically.

The Architect defines the teaching system and acceptance criteria. The Builder implements that approved system. The Reviewer independently tests whether the implementation teaches the intended learner and satisfies the specification. Reviewer evidence feeds the next bounded revision.
